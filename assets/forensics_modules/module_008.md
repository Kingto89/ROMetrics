## **Concept (Rewritten for Classroom Teaching)**

In introductory kinesiology, students are taught that most people have a **dominant limb** that is stronger and more practiced than the other.
This often leads to the assumption that the dominant arm is always the better choice for performance.

This assumption is incomplete.

Human movement performance depends on **task demands**, not simply on which limb is stronger. Different tasks prioritize different outcomes, such as:

* force production,
* speed of movement,
* ability to stop or reverse motion,
* and readiness for repeated actions.

The dominant arm typically excels at **producing high force quickly**, which makes it effective for single, powerful actions.
However, high force production often comes with greater motion after the action, requiring more time to regain the starting position.

The non-dominant arm typically produces **less force**, but often shows **greater consistency and control**, especially during repeated or cyclic movements where returning to a ready position matters.

As a result, the arm that performs best depends on **what the task requires**:

* Tasks emphasizing **maximum output** favor the dominant arm.
* Tasks emphasizing **recovery, repeatability, or readiness** may favor the non-dominant arm.

This module teaches students to evaluate limb performance based on **task goals**, rather than relying on dominance alone.
The purpose is not to label one arm as better, but to understand **why different limbs may be more effective for different movement demands**.

**Key instructional takeaway:**

> Limb dominance influences performance, but it does not determine performance across all tasks.

# **Task Overview**

### **Movement Used**

**Single-Arm Strike with Natural Recoil**

* One arm at a time
* Strike forward
* Return to guard
* Repeat for a short sequence

No targets.
No opponents.
No meters.

This is a **natural athletic motion** (boxing, football hand strikes, basketball clears).

---

# **Trial Structure**

The **movement never changes**.
Only the **task goal** changes.

## **Trial 1 — Goal: Maximum Strike Impact**

### **Goal Statement (Shown Clearly)**

> **Goal:** Deliver the most forceful single strike.

---

### **Student Action**

1. Choose one arm:

   * ☐ **Dominant arm**
   * ☐ **Non-dominant arm**
2. Run the strike sequence (3 strikes).

---

### **What the Student Sees**

* One arm produces:

  * faster extension
  * larger follow-through
  * more aggressive motion

This arm **clearly appears superior** for this goal.

---

### **Forced Decision (Required)**

> **Which arm better achieved the goal of maximum impact?**
> ☐ Dominant arm
> ☐ Non-dominant arm

---

### **Checkpoint Feedback (Minimal)**

> *This arm produced greater strike output.*

(No explanation yet.)

## **Trial 2 — Goal: Rapid Recovery & Readiness**

### **Goal Statement (Critical Shift)**

> **Goal:** Deliver repeated strikes while returning cleanly to guard each time.

Same movement.
Same body.
Same timing.

Only the **goal** changes.

---

### **Student Action**

1. Choose one arm again:

   * ☐ **Dominant arm**
   * ☐ **Non-dominant arm**
2. Run the same strike sequence.

---

### **What the Student Sees**

* One arm:

  * snaps back to guard faster
  * looks more repeatable
  * maintains readiness between strikes
* The other:

  * lingers extended
  * recovers less cleanly
  * looks powerful but less prepared

The contrast is **visually obvious**.

---

### **Forced Decision (Required)**

> **Which arm better achieved the goal of readiness and recovery?**
> ☐ Dominant arm
> ☐ Non-dominant arm

---

## **Trial 3 — Final Choice: One Arm Only**

### **Final Goal Statement**

> **Goal:** Perform a sequence of strikes where **both impact and readiness matter**.
> You may choose **only one arm**.

This mirrors real sport constraints.

---

### **Student Action**

1. Choose:

   * ☐ **Dominant arm**
   * ☐ **Non-dominant arm**
2. Run the final strike sequence.

---

### **Outcome (Visible, Not Scored)**

* Choosing based on **impact alone**:

  * strikes look powerful
  * recovery suffers
* Choosing based on **integrated reasoning**:

  * strikes slightly smaller
  * overall sequence looks more usable

No fail screen.
The consequence is **performance quality**.

## **Which Arm Is “Better” for Each Goal (NON-NEGOTIABLE)**

### **Goal: Maximum Strike Impact**

* **Better arm:** **Dominant arm**
* **Why:** higher force output and faster extension

**Animation requirements:**

* Faster forward strike
* Larger follow-through
* Slightly delayed recoil

---

### **Goal: Rapid Recovery & Readiness**

* **Better arm:** **Non-dominant arm**
* **Why:** better control and repeatability

**Animation requirements:**

* Slightly slower strike
* Shorter follow-through
* Faster, cleaner return to guard
* More consistent start/end positions

---

### **Goal: Combined Performance (Trial 3)**

* **Correct student reasoning:** choose arm based on **goal integration**, not dominance
* **Expected visual outcome:**

  * Dominant arm → powerful but sloppy sequence
  * Non-dominant arm → slightly weaker but more usable sequence

There is **no “right” arm globally**.
Only a **right arm for the goal**.


# **FL-010 — Performance Architect Capstone**

### **The Case: The "High-Effort" Cyclist**

* **The Observation (The Clue):** You see the athlete pushing the pedals, but their **knees are scrunched up** toward their chest. They tell you: *"The bike feels like it's made of lead today. I can barely push the pedals down."*
* **The Student’s Goal:** Determine if the athlete is **too weak** for the bike's resistance or if their **seat position** is making the resistance feel heavier than it is.

---

## **Bay 1: The Strength Bay (The Weight vs. Angle Test)**

**Student Instruction:** Use the Weighted Leg Press to find out if the "scrunched" position is the reason the bike feels "too heavy."

* **The Test:** Set the Leg Press to **100kg** (matching the bike's heavy resistance).
* **Step 1 (The Scrunched Test):** Put the seat **Close**. Ask the athlete to push.
* *Result:* The athlete **fails** to move the weight.


* **Step 2 (The Extended Test):** Move the seat **Back** (straightening the legs). Ask the athlete to push.
* *Result:* The athlete **easily** moves the 100kg.


* **The "Why" Checkpoint:**
* **Student Logic:** "The athlete is strong enough to move 100kg, but **only** when their legs have room. The bike feels like 'lead' because the low seat forces them into the 'failing' position I saw on the Leg Press."



---
Bay 2: The Forensic Bay (The Plank Stability Test)
Student Instruction: Use a different piece of equipment to see if the athlete can actually hold their body still.

The Equipment: Floor Mat and Neural/EMG Tablet.

The Test: Have the athlete hold a Standard Plank.

The Finding: * Abdominal EMG: The signal is "flat" or very weak.

Visual Observation: The athlete’s lower back "bows" or sags toward the floor almost immediately.

The Realistic Logic: If the athlete cannot hold a plank on the floor, they cannot hold their torso steady on a bike. On the bike, they are "cheating" by using their arms to push against the bars to stay upright because their core is failing.

The "Why" Checkpoint:

Student Logic: "The athlete failed the plank test. This proves their core is too weak to stabilize their spine, which explains why they have to 'white-knuckle' the bike handlebars just to stay balanced."
---

## **Bay 3: The Cardio Bay (The Heart/Brain Sync Test)**

**Student Instruction:** Check if the athlete’s engine (Heart) and computer (Brain) are ready for this "heavy" load.

* **The Test:** Look at the **Heart Rate** and the **EMG Rhythm** (Waveform).
* **The Evidence:** The Heart Rate is "red-lining" (too high) and the muscle signals are "shaky" (Neural Drift).
* **The "Why" Checkpoint:**
* **Student Logic:** "The athlete skipped the warm-up. Their heart and brain are in 'panic mode' trying to move the resistance, which makes them tire out in minutes."



---

## **The Final Advice Report**

**The student must present these three links to pass:**

1. **Strength Link:** Raising the seat fixes the **Mechanical Disadvantage** (proven by the Leg Press test).
2. **Forensic Link:** Bracing the core stops **Energy Leakage** (proven by the EMG stability test).
3. **Cardio Link:** A proper warm-up stops **Neural Drift** and heart rate spikes (proven by the HR monitor).

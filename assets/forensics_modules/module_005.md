
# **Module 5 — Mechanical Leverage and Movement Demand**

---

## **Concept (read first):**

**Lesson Focus:**
Students often believe that task difficulty is determined primarily by strength or load.

This lab teaches that **where a load is positioned relative to the joint can dramatically change demand — even when strength and load remain constant**.

Through direct interaction, students learn that **small positional changes can create large mechanical consequences**.

---

## **Common Student Beliefs vs Lab Evidence**

| Student Belief                  | Lab Evidence                      |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Same weight = same difficulty   | Difficulty changes immediately    |
| Strength determines effort      | Strength does not change          |
| Trying harder solves difficulty | Effort increases, control worsens |
| Position matters only a little  | Small changes have large effects  |

---

## **What This Lab Is Teaching**

This lab distinguishes between:

* **External load** vs **internal joint demand**
* **Strength** vs **mechanical leverage**
* **Effort** vs **mechanical disadvantage**

Students learn that **leverage is a primary determinant of demand**, independent of strength or motivation.

---

## **Why This Matters**

Understanding leverage explains:

* why the same weight can feel easy or impossible
* why exercise technique matters
* why joint position affects safety and control
* why “just try harder” often fails

This concept appears in **every introductory kinesiology and biomechanics course** and is foundational for all later movement analysis.

## **Trial Guide**

Follow the steps below.
Your choices directly change what happens in real time.

---

## **System Rules (Visible to Student)**

* External load is **locked**
* Strength is **unchanged**
* Speed is **locked**
* You may change **position only**

---

## **Trial 1 — Establish the Reference Condition**

### Student Action

1. Position the load where it feels **most manageable**.
2. Begin the task.

**Observe:**

* Effort level
* Control
* Ability to complete the task

This establishes your **reference experience**.

---

## **Belief Checkpoint (Required)**

> **What do you believe determines how hard this task feels?**
> ☐ The weight itself
> ☐ My strength
> ☐ Where the load is positioned
> ☐ Not sure

You must select one to continue.

---

## **Trial 2 — Student-Initiated Change**

### Student Action

Choose **one** adjustment:

* ☐ Move the load **farther from the joint**
* ☐ Move the load **closer to the joint**
* ☐ Change **nothing**

---

### **Immediate System Response**

* **Farther from joint**

  * Effort increases immediately
  * Control decreases
  * Tremor or instability appears

* **Closer to joint**

  * Effort decreases immediately
  * Control improves
  * Task feels easier

* **No change**

  * Effort and control remain unchanged

> The displayed load value does **not** change.

---

## **Trial 3 — Error Is Allowed**

### Student Action

Choose **one**:

* ☐ Increase speed (“try harder”)
* ☐ Change posture without moving the load
* ☐ Reposition the load

---

### **Immediate Consequence**

* **Increase speed**

  * Effort increases further
  * Control worsens

* **Posture change only**

  * Minimal improvement
  * Effort remains high

* **Reposition the load**

  * Effort drops immediately
  * Control returns

This proves that **strategy cannot override leverage**.

---

## **Trial 4 — Precision Test**

### System State

* Posture and speed are **locked**

### Student Action

1. Move the load in **small increments only**.
2. Observe how each change affects effort.

**Observe:**

* Large effort changes from small position changes
* No change in load or strength

---

## **Final Comparison Step**

> **Which action most reliably changed task difficulty?**

☐ Trying harder
☐ Moving faster
☐ Changing posture
☐ Changing leverage

(Student must select one to complete the lab.)

---

## **Key Inference**

> **The load did not change.
> Strength did not change.
> Task difficulty changed only when leverage changed.**

## **Developer Implementation Notes (Not Student-Facing)**

### **Core Requirements**

* Single avatar
* Fixed external load value (UI-visible)
* Locked speed and strength
* Load position adjustable along a defined arc or axis

---

### **Mechanical Response Logic**

* Internal joint demand scales with distance from joint
* Effort, tremor, and control respond **immediately** to leverage changes
* Speed increases amplify demand but never resolve it

---

### **Critical Constraints**

* Never imply weakness or fitness level
* Never label actions as “correct” or “incorrect”
* Evidence must appear visually and immediately
* Load value must remain constant on screen

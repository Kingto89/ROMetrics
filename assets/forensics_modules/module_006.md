

# **Module 6 — Agonist vs Antagonist Interaction**

---

## **Concept (read first):**

**Lesson Focus:**
Students often assume that effort and difficulty are determined by strength or load.

This lab teaches that **unnecessary opposing muscle activity can dramatically increase effort**, even when the task is mechanically easy.

The student is not told what the issue is at first.
They must **discover the cause through interaction**.

---

## **Common Student Beliefs vs Lab Evidence**

| Student Belief                        | Lab Evidence                         |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| “This is hard because I’m weak”       | Load is light and unchanged          |
| “Trying harder will fix it”           | Effort increases further             |
| “Position or speed must be wrong”     | Adjustments don’t resolve difficulty |
| “Something I’m doing is causing this” | Relaxation immediately restores ease |

---

## **What This Lab Is Teaching**

This lab distinguishes between:

* **Strength** vs **muscle coordination**
* **Effort** vs **resistance created by the body**
* **Prime movement** vs **opposing muscle activity**

Students learn that **how muscles interact matters as much as how strong they are**.

---

## **Why This Matters**

Understanding agonist–antagonist interaction explains:

* stiffness during movement
* shakiness with light loads
* early fatigue despite low demand
* why “relaxing” can improve performance

This concept is **universal in first-year kinesiology** and foundational for later topics in biomechanics, rehab, and sport.

---

## **Trial Guide**

You will complete **three trials**.
Your actions directly change task difficulty in real time.

---

## **System Rules (Visible to Student)**

* Load is **light and fixed**
* Speed is **slow and fixed**
* Lever position is **favorable and fixed**
* You may change **how you move**, not the task

---

## **Trial 1 — The Unexpected Difficulty**

### Student Action

1. Attempt to complete the task.

### What Happens

* The task feels harder than expected
* Movement feels stiff or shaky
* Early difficulty or failure may occur

No explanation is provided.

---

### **Checkpoint — Student Hypothesis**

> **Why does this feel harder than it should?**
> ☐ The load is heavier than shown
> ☐ I’m not strong enough
> ☐ Something about how I’m moving
> ☐ The simulation is incorrect

(Student must select one.)

---

## **Trial 2 — Free Exploration (Discovery Phase)**

### Student Action

You may change **anything you want** except:

* load
* speed
* leverage

Most students will:

* try harder
* brace or stiffen
* change posture
* move faster

### Immediate Evidence

* Trying harder → effort increases
* Bracing → stiffness and shakiness increase
* Posture changes → little improvement

A subtle live indicator appears:

**Opposing Tension**

* Increases when the student stiffens or braces
* Decreases when the student relaxes

No definition is given.

---

## **Trial 3 — Control the Hidden Variable**

### Student Action

1. Intentionally increase stiffness → observe effort increase.
2. Intentionally relax → observe effort decrease.

### What Happens

* Difficulty toggles **on / off** in real time
* Load and speed never change
* When opposing tension drops:

  * Movement becomes smooth
  * Effort drops immediately
  * Task completes successfully

---

## **Concept Reveal (After Trial 3)**

> *Opposing muscles were resisting the movement.*
> *This is called **agonist–antagonist co-contraction**.*

---

## **Key Inference (Required)**

> **What actually caused the difficulty?**

☐ Lack of strength
☐ Load magnitude
☐ Opposing muscle activity
☐ Movement speed

**Correct Answer:** ☐ Opposing muscle activity

---

## **Post-Trial Questions**

**Q1**
Why did the task feel difficult even though the load was light?

A. Strength was insufficient
B. The load increased
C. Opposing muscles resisted the movement
D. Balance was lost

**Correct Answer:** C

---

**Q2**
Why did “trying harder” make the task worse?

A. Fatigue increased instantly
B. Strength decreased
C. Co-contraction increased resistance
D. Speed was too slow

**Correct Answer:** C

---

**Q3**
Which change most effectively reduced effort?

A. Increasing force
B. Moving faster
C. Relaxing opposing muscles
D. Changing posture

**Correct Answer:** C

---

## **WHAT THE LAB PROVES**

| Student Action           | Immediate Evidence |
| ------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Braces / stiffens        | Effort spikes      |
| Tries harder             | Task worsens       |
| Adjusts posture only     | Minimal change     |
| Relaxes opposing tension | Task becomes easy  |

---

## **Developer Implementation Notes (Not Student-Facing)**

### **Core Requirements**

* Single avatar
* Fixed external load (clearly light)
* Fixed speed and leverage
* Real-time **Opposing Tension** meter

---

### **Opposing Tension Logic**

* Meter rises with:

  * simultaneous activation of opposing muscle groups
  * excessive stiffness or bracing
* Meter falls with:

  * relaxation
  * smoother coordination

Difficulty, tremor, and control scale directly with this value.
